## The context

**It all starts with a problem.**

Every developer needs a place to exist online. When I started freelancing, I didn't have one — so I built my first personal portfolio. At the time, I presented myself as a software developer specialized in Flutter, Django, Next.js and Android. rahimdev.me is that first attempt: the site that taught me how to design and build for myself.

## The challenge

**The challenge: build a first portfolio that feels like me.**

Looking back, the real challenge wasn't technical — it was figuring out what I wanted to say about myself. What to show, what to leave out, and how to make a personal site feel professional without losing personality. That first version was a learning ground as much as a portfolio.

## The approach

**So how to solve this?**

I focused on the essentials: a strong hero, a clear about section, a few featured projects, and a simple way to get in touch. The goal was a site that would load fast, look serious, and let people understand who I was in a few seconds — nothing more. It was deliberately simple, and that simplicity is what made it work.

The interface was built around strong typography, generous spacing, and subtle animations to give the site a sense of motion. A dark and a light theme were both included, along with a custom cursor and smooth scrolling. It was ambitious for a first portfolio — and honestly, looking back, it tried to do a bit too much at once.

## Important decisions

Some decisions that changed the experience.

- **A bold, animated hero.** The site opens with movement and personality, to make a strong first impression.
- **A theme toggle from the start.** Dark and light modes were treated as equal, long before it became a default expectation.
- **Motion as a signature.** GSAP and smooth scrolling gave the site a rhythm that made it feel more crafted than a typical first portfolio.

## Development

**The design was ready. Now it had to be brought to life.**

The site was built as a modern Next.js application with a clean component structure. It was an opportunity to put into practice everything I had learned about front-end architecture, animation and deployment, on a project that was truly mine.

The project is open source and available on GitHub: [github.com/Rahim10020/rahimdev.me](https://github.com/Rahim10020/rahimdev.me)

The live website is available at: [rahim-ali-dev.vercel.app](https://rahim-ali-dev.vercel.app/)

**And finally, it looks like this.**

A first portfolio that did its job: it introduced me, showcased my work, and gave me a place to send potential clients. It also, quietly, became the starting point of everything that followed.

## What I learned

This project taught me that a portfolio is never really finished — it evolves with the person behind it. Building rahimdev.me helped me understand what I actually enjoy doing: designing interfaces and crafting front-end experiences. That realization is what led me to reposition myself as a designer and front-end developer, and to build a new portfolio that reflects who I am today.
