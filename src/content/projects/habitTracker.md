## The context

**It all starts with a problem.**

Building a new habit is easy for a few days — and hard for a few weeks. Most people give up not because they lack motivation, but because they lose sight of their progress. R HabitTracker was created to make that progress visible, day after day, in a calm and encouraging way.

## The challenge

**The challenge: motivate without pressure.**

Tracking habits can quickly feel like a chore. The real challenge was to design an experience that celebrates small wins, makes consistency visible, and gently reminds people to show up — without ever feeling like another task on the list.

## The approach

**So how to solve this?**

Instead of overwhelming users with data, the app focuses on what matters: the habits themselves, the days they were completed, and the streaks they build. Progress is shown visually through a heatmap and a calendar, reminders are gentle, and achievements turn consistency into something to look forward to. Everything works offline, so the app is always available when a habit is due.

## UX

**Before we think about pixels, let's think about the journey.**

![R HabitTracker UX — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

The journey is built around a simple daily loop: open the app, see today's habits, mark them as done, and watch progress grow over time. Creating a habit takes seconds, and checking in takes even less — so the app never stands between the user and the routine they're building.

## UI Design

**Once the route was clear, we could finally talk about the interface.**

![R HabitTracker UI design — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

The interface is warm and encouraging, with soft colors, expressive icons and a clear sense of rhythm. A light and a dark theme are both included and remembered across sessions, so the app feels personal from the first launch.

## Important decisions

Some decisions that changed the experience.

- **Celebrate, don't punish.** Achievements, streaks and confetti are there to reward consistency, never to shame a missed day.
- **Show progress, not numbers.** A heatmap and a calendar make consistency visible at a glance, without drowning users in statistics.
- **Reminders that respect attention.** Local notifications are scheduled around the user's own routine, not the app's.
- **Offline by default.** Everything is stored on the device, so habits and progress are always available — even without a connection.

## Development

**The design was ready. Now it had to be brought to life.**

![R HabitTracker Development — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

The app was built as a single, cross-platform product so it could run smoothly on both Android and iOS. Special care was put into two areas that define the experience: reliable local reminders that fire on time, and a fast, private data layer that keeps every habit and every completion safely on the device.

The project is open source and available on GitHub: [github.com/Rahim10020/HabitTracker-Mkoko-](https://github.com/Rahim10020/HabitTracker-Mkoko-)

## The result

**And finally, it looks like this.**

![R HabitTracker result — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

A focused habit tracker that does one thing well: help people show up for themselves, a little every day, and see how far they've come.

## What I learned

Building R HabitTracker taught me that motivation is a design problem, not a feature. The smallest details — a well-timed reminder, a satisfying celebration, a clear view of progress — matter far more than a long list of options. It also reinforced how powerful a calm, well-paced interface can be when the goal is to change a daily behavior.
