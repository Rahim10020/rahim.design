## The context

**It all starts with a problem.**

Staying focused is hard when everything is scattered. Tasks live in one app, timers in another, progress is invisible, and motivation fades. Focusly was created to bring the essentials of a productive day together — what to do, when to focus, and how far you've come — into one calm workspace.

## The challenge

**The challenge: stay simple while being ambitious.**

Focusly brings together task management, recurring tasks, a Pomodoro timer, streaks, insights and achievements. The real challenge was to make all of this feel like a single, coherent experience — powerful when needed, but never overwhelming.

## The approach

**So how to solve this?**

Instead of treating each feature as its own world, everything revolves around a simple daily loop: plan your tasks, focus on them in timed sessions, and watch your progress build over time. Insights and achievements are there to encourage, not to distract, and everything is designed so users can start in seconds and grow into the app as they go.

## UX

**Before we think about pixels, let's think about the journey.**

![Focusly UX — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

The journey follows a natural rhythm: set up an account, see your workspace, add what matters for today, start a focus session, and check your progress at the end of the week. Each part of the app answers one clear question — _what should I do now?_ — and stays out of the way the rest of the time.

## UI Design

**Once the route was clear, we could finally talk about the interface.**

![Focusly UI design — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

The interface is clean and structured, built around a clear visual hierarchy and a calm palette that keeps attention on the work itself. A light and a dark theme are both included, and the layout adapts smoothly from wide desktop screens to smaller ones.

## Important decisions

Some decisions that changed the experience.

- **One workspace, not many tools.** Tasks, focus sessions and insights live together, so users don't have to juggle between apps.
- **Encourage, don't pressure.** Streaks, achievements and insights are designed to motivate, never to guilt.
- **Privacy and control by default.** Accounts are personal, data belongs to the user, and exporting it is a first-class feature — not an afterthought.
- **A calm interface.** Visual noise was removed everywhere it could be, so focus stays on the task at hand.

## Development

**The design was ready. Now it had to be brought to life.**

![Focusly Development — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

Focusly was built as a modern web application, designed to feel fast, responsive and reliable on any device. Considerable care was put into the parts users depend on most: real-time sync across sessions, dependable reminders and timers, and a data layer that keeps every task and every focus session safe.

The project is open source and available on GitHub: [github.com/Rahim10020/focusly](https://github.com/Rahim10020/focusly)

## The result

**And finally, it looks like this.**

![Focusly result — à remplacer](/images/others/image_placeholder.svg)

A single, focused workspace that turns intentions into daily practice — and shows, day after day, how much has actually been accomplished.

## What I learned

Building Focusly taught me that ambition and simplicity can coexist. The more features were added, the more important it became to hide them behind a clear, calm experience. It also confirmed that a well-designed daily loop — plan, focus, reflect — can change how people work far more than any single feature.
